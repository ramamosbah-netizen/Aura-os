| XOP-12 | Signature evidence | COMPLETE | SIGNATURE EVIDENCE THAT NAMES ITS SIGNATORY, COVERS WHAT WAS SIGNED, PRINTS ON THE DOCUMENT AND CANNOT BE REWRITTEN — on all four surfaces the frozen evidence names. Every one of them offered a pad and threw the stroke away: the daily report held it in React state the payload never read, the handover acceptance bound it to `onChange={() => {}}`, the commissioning sign-off had two free-text names and two blank ruled lines beneath a note describing “the witnessed sign-off”, and the inspection request had no upload route at all because quality had none of any kind. WHO SIGNED IS NOT WHO RECORDED IT. `capturedBy`/`createdBy` is the account that pressed the button; the daily report printed “Signed by” that account over a signature the FOREMAN gave. Every surface now carries a separate signatory label, refused rather than defaulted — the domain THROWS instead of falling back, because a silent fallback is the defect and not a milder version of it — and no document presents one as the other. A SIGNATURE COVERS A RESULT, NOT A ROW ID: a content hash computed server-side from the persisted record, answering current / superseded / unverifiable, so a record reworked afterwards cannot keep printing a signature beneath figures nobody agreed to. SELECTION ASKS WHAT THE FILE IS: the daily report used to pick its signature with a regex over the uploader’s own free-text description while filing photographs and signatures alike as `progress`, so a photo described “riser sign-off” printed AS the signature on a controlled document. AND THE BYTES ARE SEALED. `DEFAULT_OWNER_POLICY` gives a creator EDIT, and the creator of a signature is the person who RECORDED the act — so the generic policy handed the recorder EDIT on the very signature that constrains them, and the generic version route would have replaced it. |
